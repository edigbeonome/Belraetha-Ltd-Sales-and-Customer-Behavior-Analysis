Project Overview

This project analyses the dbo.ORDERS table in the Belraetha Ltd database using Microsoft SQL Server. It examines sales and profit performance, compares regions and product categories, looks at customer-segment purchasing patterns, and identifies high-value transactions and the most profitable product sub-categories.

The SQL queries aggregate and filter order-level records to produce information that can support commercial and operational decisions.

Business Questions
What are the overall sales and profit generated by the orders?
Which sales region contributes the greatest sales value?
How do the main product categories compare in sales and profit?
Which customer segment has the highest average sales per order record?
How many orders have sales greater than $1,000, and what do they indicate?
Which product sub-categories generate the highest total profit?
Which categories and sub-categories may need closer management attention?
Dataset

The data lives in the dbo.ORDERS table. It combines order, customer, geographic, shipping, product, and financial information.

Fields visible in the query results:

Data Quality Notes

The analysis began with a structure check: SELECT TOP 10 * FROM ORDERS.

No explicit cleaning steps (duplicate removal, NULL handling, label standardisation, data-type correction, outlier treatment) were documented, so none are claimed as completed. Before using the results for production decisions, check for:

duplicate order lines
missing customer or product values
invalid dates
non-numeric sales or profit values
inconsistent category, region, or ship-mode labels

The date range and row count should also be documented.

SQL Queries and Key Findings
1. Overall sales and profit
sql
SELECT
    SUM(SALES)  AS TOTALSALES,
    SUM(PROFIT) AS TOTALPROFIT
FROM ORDERS;
Metric	Value (USD)
Total sales	$2,297,201.07
Total profit	$286,397.79

The business is profitable overall. Profit is about 12.47% of sales. This is a descriptive ratio, not a net margin, unless the Profit field already includes all relevant costs.

Correction to the original Word report: the report and the SQL comment in the source query give the totals as $22,972,011.07 and $2,863,997.79. The query result grid shows 2297201.06792814 and 286397.789748728, and the four regional sales figures below sum to about $2,297,201. This README uses the values from the result grid. The profit-to-sales ratio is unaffected.

2. Sales by region
sql
SELECT Region, SUM(SALES) AS TOTALSALES
FROM ORDERS
GROUP BY Region
ORDER BY Region DESC;
Rank	Region	Total sales (USD)
1	West	$725,457.93
2	East	$678,781.36
3	Central	$501,239.88
4	South	$391,721.90

West leads and South trails. West sales are about 85% higher than South, which is a meaningful geographic gap.

The query orders by Region, not by sales. To list regions by sales, use ORDER BY TOTALSALES DESC.

3. Sales and profit by category
sql
SELECT CATEGORY,
       SUM(SALES)  AS TOTALSALES,
       SUM(PROFIT) AS TOTALPROFIT
FROM ORDERS
GROUP BY CATEGORY;
Category	Total sales (USD)	Total profit (USD)	Profit / sales
Technology	$836,154.10	$145,455.66	~17.40%
Office Supplies	$719,046.99	$122,490.88	~17.04%
Furniture	$741,999.81	$18,451.25	~2.49%

Technology leads on both sales and profit. Furniture has sales close to Office Supplies but a much lower profit, making it the clearest profitability concern.

4. Average sales by customer segment
sql
SELECT SEGMENT, AVG(SALES) AS AVG_SALES
FROM ORDERS
GROUP BY Segment;
Segment	Average sales per order row (USD)
Home Office	$240.97
Corporate	$233.82
Consumer	$223.73

The differences are modest. This is an average per row, not necessarily average order value if one order spans several rows, and it is not customer lifetime value.

5. High-value orders
sql
SELECT *
FROM ORDERS
WHERE SALES > 1000;

The query returned 468 records. The visible rows span Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office customers across many U.S. states, mostly shipped via Standard Class. Some customers (for example Becky Martin and Dianna Wilson) appear more than once, which hints at repeat high-value buyers. A grouped customer-level query is needed to confirm this.

6. Top 5 most profitable sub-categories
sql
SELECT TOP 5 Sub_Category, SUM(PROFIT) AS TOTALPROFIT
FROM ORDERS
GROUP BY Sub_Category
ORDER BY TOTALPROFIT DESC;
Rank	Sub-category	Total profit (USD)
1	Copiers	$55,617.90
2	Phones	$44,516.25
3	Accessories	$41,936.78
4	Paper	$34,053.34
5	Binders	$30,221.64

The top three are all Technology items. Paper and Binders show that Office Supplies also contributes strongly.

Part of this query was hidden by a tooltip in the screenshot. The GROUP BY and ORDER BY lines above are reconstructed from the output and the query comment.

7. Categories with profit above $10,000
sql
SELECT Category, SUM(PROFIT) AS TOTALPROFIT
FROM ORDERS
GROUP BY Category
HAVING SUM(PROFIT) > 10000;

All three categories passed the threshold: Technology (~$145.5K), Office Supplies (~$122.5K), and Furniture (~$18.5K). The filter removed nothing, and Furniture remains the weakest category.

Business Insights
Profitable overall. About $286K profit on about $2.30M sales, a baseline to validate against source data and the definition of Profit.
Uneven regional sales. West and East lead, South is weakest. The queries do not explain why (demand, coverage, product mix, or execution could all play a part).
Technology is the strongest category in both sales and profit.
Furniture needs a profitability review. Substantial sales but very low profit. Discounting, procurement, fulfilment, returns, or product mix could be factors, but none are tested yet.
Segment differences are small. Home Office is highest, but segment-specific offers should be judged on conversion and repeat purchases.
High-value transactions matter. 468 records above $1,000, with repeat names suggesting customer-level targeting may pay off.
Copiers, Phones, and Accessories are the leading profit sub-categories.
Recommendations
Investigate Furniture profitability first. Review discount levels, supplier costs, delivery and handling costs, returns, and profit by Furniture sub-category before making broad pricing changes.
Protect and grow Technology. Keep stock available, monitor stock-outs, and test bundles or complementary offers.
Replicate effective practices from stronger regions. Compare product mix, segment mix, order value, discounting, and sales coverage between West/East and South.
Develop a high-value customer programme. Analyse total spend, order counts, and time between purchases per customer, then consider account management or loyalty offers.
Use sub-category profitability in merchandising and inventory decisions. Track margin, not just sales.
Validate and extend the analysis. Run data-quality checks, then add monthly trends, discounts, shipping costs, order counts, and profit by region, segment, and sub-category.
Conclusion

The analysis shows positive aggregate profitability, with Technology leading categories, West leading regional sales, and Home Office showing the highest average sales per order row. The key issue for further investigation is Furniture's low profit despite substantial sales. Copiers, Phones, and Accessories are the strongest sub-category profit contributors.

These findings are a practical starting point for improving product mix, regional execution, customer targeting, and margin management, and should be refined once data-quality checks and further driver analysis are done.
